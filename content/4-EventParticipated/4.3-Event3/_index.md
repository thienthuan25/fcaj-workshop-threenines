# Enterprise Cloud Architectures and Industry Application featuring Cloud Kinetics & Renova

Although I was unable to participate in this event because there were not enough registration slots, fortunately, the event was livestreamed directly on YouTube, so I was able to follow the event and learn many things from it.

### Purpose of the Event

The **AWS: Enterprise Cloud Architectures and Industry Application** event was a practical visit for students of **Swinburne Vietnam**, especially the Can Tho campus, to the AWS office. The program focused on sharing practical knowledge about cloud computing and career orientation in the IT industry.

### List of Speakers

- **Nguyễn Trần Minh Duy** - Industry Liaison Officer at Swinburne Vietnam.
- **Nguyễn Gia Hưng** - Head of Solutions Architect for Vietnam & Cambodia.
- **Bành Cảm Vĩnh** - Data Engineer at Renova Cloud.
- **Như Trần** - Account Manager at Amazon Web Services Vietnam.
- **Nguyễn Ngọc Quỳnh Mai** - Cloud Operations Engineer at VietNam International Bank (VIB).
- **Khang Nguyễn** - Solution Architect at CloudKinetics.

### Content

#### Introduction and Connection - Nguyễn Ngọc Quỳnh Mai & Nguyễn Trần Minh Duy

The event marked the third consecutive year that **Swinburne Vietnam** and **AWS Vietnam** collaborated to organize a practical visit for students. It created a bridge for students to gain closer access to a professional technology environment while opening opportunities to learn from experts working in the cloud computing field at AWS.

#### Topic 1: The Current State of the IT Labor Market and Career Mindset - Nguyễn Gia Hưng

- Cloud market demand in Vietnam: Mr. Hưng shared about the strong growth of cloud computing technology, with large organizations such as banks and financial institutions increasingly moving their systems to the Cloud. Many international corporations are building **Talent Hubs** in Vietnam, creating a very high demand for high-quality IT human resources.

- Infrastructure investment: He analyzed the enormous scale of Cloud infrastructure investment in Vietnam, with costs reaching billions of dollars and a long payback period of 15 to 20 years, affirming AWS's long-term commitment to building the future workforce.

- Recruitment paradox: The market has a clear gap between demand for **Junior** and **Senior** employees. Mr. Hưng emphasized that students need to improve their practical skills instead of focusing only on academic knowledge, because current business requirements have become much higher.

- **Mindset about AI in learning:** He warned against overusing AI to complete assignments. Instead, students should use AI as an assistant to solve complex problems and meet real business needs. For example, projects need to ensure **scalability**, **security**, chatbot integration, and recommendation features instead of only building simple websites as before.

#### Topic 2: The Reality of Data Engineering and the Difference Between Academic and Enterprise Environments - Bành Cảm Vĩnh

- The difference between school assignments and real-world environments: Mr. Vĩnh pointed out that school projects often have cleaned data, clear requirements, flexible timelines, and a small scale. Meanwhile, enterprise environments require the ability to process large volumes of real data, deal with frequently changing requirements, think about **architecture**, and take direct responsibility for the company's business performance.

- Mindset about tools and architecture: He emphasized that tools such as AWS and OpenStack can change continuously, but the core system architecture remains unchanged. Students need to focus on mastering fundamental knowledge rather than only memorizing how to use a specific tool.

- The role of a Data Engineer in an enterprise: It is not only about writing code. Data Engineering professionals need to deeply understand the **business** in order to communicate with departments, define accurate requirements, and bring real value to the enterprise instead of only building technical systems.

- Solving problems when requirements are unclear: When facing customers who frequently change requirements, instead of reacting passively, engineers need to establish an alignment process among departments to ensure that every request has practical value and optimizes resources.

- Results-oriented mindset: Mr. Vĩnh advised students to approach problems from the desired outcome first in order to avoid **over-engineering**.

#### Topic 3: Overcoming Fear and the Art of Workplace Communication - Nguyễn Ngọc Quỳnh Mai & Như Trần

- Facing personal fears: Ms. Như encouraged students to identify their real fears instead of only generally saying that they are "afraid of making mistakes."

- Communication: She shared her own fear of public speaking and how she overcame it by volunteering to speak at many forums to practice her skills, thereby becoming calmer and more confident.

- Building connections: To reduce the distance with managers, she advised young people not to avoid them but to actively create a presence through small actions such as greeting them in the morning or offering to support work. Proactive communication helps managers recognize your effort and potential.

- Communication at work, especially with customers, is not only about conveying information but also about knowing when to compromise and when to stand firm in order to protect an effective work process. She emphasized that it is not necessary to have an answer immediately, but it is necessary to have a willing attitude and responsibility in finding an answer afterward.

- She also affirmed that real opportunities do not only come from public job postings, but also from networking through sharing sessions, relationships, and small things that others often overlook.

#### Topic 4: Sharing Practical Experience - Khang Nguyễn

- Mr. Khang emphasized that although opportunities to access technology and documentation are easier today, the biggest challenge is the trend of overusing AI to complete assignments without understanding the technical fundamentals.

- Learning methods to be ready for work:
  
  + Always ask "Why?" instead of only learning by rote.
  + Mistakes are an inevitable part of the learning process; the important thing is to analyze why you made a mistake.
  + Always proactively learn beyond expectations and explore modules taught in class more deeply to create a difference during interviews.

- Mr. Khang shared five important factors that businesses look for in a candidate, in order of priority: Attitude, Qualifications, Experience, Exposure, and Aptitude.

#### What I Learned

**1.** Clearly understand the difference between academics and reality:

Projects at school often have clean data environments and static requirements, while enterprises require the ability to handle large volumes of real data, frequently changing requirements, and the ability to think about the overall system architecture instead of only knowing how to use tools.

**2.** Instead of making technology unnecessarily complex, engineers need to prioritize business objectives. Every technical solution must bring practical value to the enterprise.

**3.** Soft skills:

- Communication is not only for extroverted people. You need to work with many departments and understand the **business** in order to implement projects effectively.

- Opportunities do not come by themselves; we need to actively build connections through sharing sessions and small actions in daily work.

**4.** Fear of making mistakes is a normal feeling, but you need to identify the fear instead of avoiding it. Accepting mistakes and analyzing why you made them is the fastest way to grow.

**5.** Although AI provides significant support in our daily lives, we need to avoid overusing it to do work for us without understanding its fundamentals. The secret to success lies in always asking questions and maintaining a mindset that is always ready to learn and overcome personal challenges in the future.
